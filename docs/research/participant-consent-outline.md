# Participant Consent Outline

Version: 0.1  
Status: draft for legal/privacy/accessibility review  
Last updated: 2026-08-08

This is a plain-language outline, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal or ethics review. Do not use it as a signed form until reviewed and adapted by the project owner and qualified counsel/research advisor.

## What this is

We are designing AccessForge, an early-stage tool that may help people describe an access difficulty with an everyday object and explore possible low-risk assistive adapters. We are asking for your experience so the product reflects what people actually need.

## What will happen

You may be asked to:

- describe an everyday object or interaction
- react to prototype screens and wording
- explain what currently works or does not work
- suggest what a useful adapter would need to do
- discuss what would make a design trustworthy or unacceptable

You do not need to demonstrate a painful, tiring, embarrassing, or unsafe action. You can take breaks or stop at any time.

## Optional activities

Each of these must have its own yes/no choice:

- audio recording
- video recording
- still photographs
- transcription
- anonymous quotation
- use of an anonymized insight in public documentation
- future contact about testing
- contribution of a design or measurement to a public dataset

Saying no to any optional activity does not affect participation or payment.

## What we will not do

- We will not diagnose you or evaluate your body.
- We will not promise that AccessForge can create a useful device.
- We will not ask you to test an automatically generated physical device during this planning phase.
- We will not send your media or personal information to an AI provider without a separate explanation and consent.
- We will not use your information to train models or publish it by default.

## Possible risks

Talking about an access difficulty may feel private or frustrating. A prototype may misunderstand your goal. Recording or storage may create a privacy risk. You can skip questions, correct our notes, stop the session, or ask us to delete research materials as described below.

If we learn about a use case that could cause harm, we may stop discussing a design and explain why. This is a safety boundary, not a judgment about your need.

## Possible benefits

You may not receive a device or direct personal benefit. Your experience may influence product scope, wording, accessibility, safety rules, and whether a feature is built at all.

## Payment and expenses

You will be paid [amount/currency] for the session, whether or not you complete every activity. Payment will be made [method/timing]. We will reimburse [pre-approved accessibility expenses] according to the agreed process. Payment details are stored separately from research notes where practical.

## Privacy and retention

We will use a participant code in research notes. Contact/payment information is kept separately with restricted access. Research materials will be deleted by [date/retention period] unless you separately agree to longer retention. We will tell you which files were retained and how to request deletion.

We cannot promise that a quote or anonymized insight can never be re-identified, so you may choose not to permit public quotation.

## Withdrawal

You may withdraw before or after the session by contacting [contact]. You do not need to give a reason. We will delete identifiable research materials that have not already been irreversibly anonymized or included in an aggregate analysis, subject to the reviewed retention policy.

## Questions and complaints

Contact [research lead] at [contact]. For privacy concerns, contact [privacy owner]. For complaints independent of the research team, contact [independent route].

## Confirmation checklist

The final consent form should ask the participant to confirm:

- I understand the purpose and early-stage nature of the project.
- I know participation is voluntary and I can stop.
- I know which activities are optional.
- I understand the risks, payment, retention, and withdrawal process.
- I had a chance to ask questions and receive accessible answers.
- I choose separately whether each optional activity is allowed.
- I receive a copy of this information and my choices.
